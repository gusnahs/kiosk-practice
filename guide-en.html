How to use Korean kiosks

Cafes, burger shops and even hospitals in Korea use touch-screen kiosks. This guide explains the steps and the Korean words on the screen, so you can order with confidence.

Practice on the simulator

5 things to know first

  • Look for a language button. Many kiosks, especially at big chains, have one on the start screen. Look for ‘English’, ‘Language’, ‘언어’ or a globe icon 🌐, often in a corner. Not every kiosk has one, so the word list below helps.
  • Cards are the easiest way to pay. Many kiosks take cards only. If you want to pay in cash, ask at the counter.
  • Overseas cards usually work, but not always. Visa and Mastercard are widely accepted. If your card is rejected, try another card or order at the counter.
  • Leave the card in until the screen tells you. Insert it the way the picture next to the slot shows, and wait until you see ‘카드를 빼 주세요’ (remove your card).
  • You can always go back. Most screens have ‘이전’ (back) and ‘처음으로’ (start over). If you are stuck, staff are usually nearby. Say ‘도와주세요’ (dowajuseyo, please help).

1. Ordering at a cafe

Most cafe kiosks follow the same order. The Korean on each button is shown in brackets.

  1. Tap the screen to startTap anywhere on the start screen, or tap the ‘주문하기’ (order) button.
  2. Choose eat here or take out‘매장’ means eat here and ‘포장’ means take out. Some kiosks ask this at the end instead.
  3. Pick a drinkMenus are split into tabs such as ‘커피’ (coffee), ‘음료’ (drinks) and ‘디저트’ (desserts). Items marked ‘품절’ are sold out.
  4. Choose the optionsA window opens for HOT (따뜻한) or ICE (차가운), size, an extra shot (샷 추가) or syrup. Options marked ‘필수’ (required) must be chosen. Then tap ‘담기’ (add to cart).
  5. Check your order and payTap ‘결제하기’ (pay). If you are asked about points (적립) or coupons and do not need them, tap ‘건너뛰기’ (skip) or ‘안 함’. A dessert offer may pop up too: ‘필요 없어요’ or ‘선택 안 함’ means no thanks.
  6. Pay by cardChoose ‘카드 결제’ (pay by card), insert your card and wait. If you are asked about installments (할부), choose ‘일시불’ (pay in full).
  7. Take your receipt and order numberChoose whether to print a receipt (영수증 출력). Remember your order number (주문번호). It is shown on a screen or called out when your drink is ready.

2. Ordering at a burger shop

Fast-food kiosks add a few more choices, mainly about meals.

  1. Burger only or a meal‘단품’ is the burger only. ‘세트’ is a meal with a side (사이드) and a drink (음료). Some shops also offer a large meal (라지 세트 or 큰 세트).
  2. Choose the side and the drinkFries (감자튀김) and cola (콜라) are usually the default. Some choices cost extra, and the price difference is shown on the button.
  3. Change ingredients if you wantLook for a button such as ‘재료 변경’ or ‘재료 바꾸기’. ‘빼기’ means without (for example, 양파 빼기 = no onion), and ‘추가’ means extra.
  4. Say no to extra offersAfter you add a burger, a dessert or upgrade offer often appears. Tap ‘선택 안 함’ (do not choose) or ‘아니요’ (no) to skip it.
  5. Pay and wait for your numberPay by card as at a cafe. For take-out, watch the order screen: your number moves from ‘준비 중’ (preparing) to ‘완료’ or ‘준비 완료’ (ready). Some restaurants give you a buzzer (진동벨) instead. You may be asked to enter its number on the kiosk, and you pick up your food when it vibrates.

3. Hospital kiosks

Many hospitals and clinics in Korea have kiosks for check-in (접수), payment (수납) and printing papers (증명서 발급). A few things are different for visitors from abroad:

  • Bring your ID. Hospitals in Korea check your identity, so bring your passport or residence card (외국인등록증).
  • On your first visit, go to the desk. Kiosks usually ask for a Korean registration number, or your birth date and phone number. If you are new to the hospital, it is easier to register at the reception desk (원무과 or 접수).
  • Kiosks help on later visits and for payment. Once you are registered, you can check in, pay and print papers at a kiosk.
  • Take the prescription to a pharmacy. A prescription (처방전) is used at a pharmacy (약국). In Korea it is usually valid for 3 days from the issue date, so check the date printed on it.
  • In an emergency, call 119. Travelers can call the Korea Travel Hotline 1330 for information and interpretation in English.

Typical check-in steps

  1. Choose ‘접수’ (check-in) on the first screen.
  2. Choose a first visit (처음 방문 or 초진) or a return visit (재진).
  3. Confirm your identity (본인 확인) by entering numbers or scanning an ID card.
  4. Choose your symptoms or a department (진료과), such as 내과 (internal medicine).
  5. Check the details, take your number ticket (번호표) and wait until your number is called.

6. When something goes wrong

My card was rejected.
Some kiosks accept only Korean cards. Try another card, or order and pay at the counter.
I cannot find English.
Not every kiosk has a language button. Use the word list above, or ask staff for help.
The kiosk asks for a phone number.
This is usually for points (적립). You can skip it with ‘건너뛰기’ or ‘안 함’.
I chose the wrong thing.
Use ‘이전’ (back) or ‘처음으로’ (start over). In the cart you can delete items with ‘삭제’ or ‘빼기’.
The screen went back to the start.
Kiosks reset if you do nothing for a while. Just start again.
