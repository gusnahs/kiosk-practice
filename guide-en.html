How to use Korean kiosks

Cafes, burger shops, hospitals, airport buses, subway and train stations in Korea all use touch-screen kiosks. This guide explains the steps and the Korean words on the screen, so you can order with confidence.

Practice on the simulator

5 things to know first

  • Look for a language button. Many kiosks, especially at big chains, have one on the start screen. Look for ‘English’, ‘Language’, ‘언어’ or a globe icon 🌐, often in a corner. Not every kiosk has one, so the word list below helps.
  • Cards are the easiest way to pay. Many kiosks take cards only. If you want to pay in cash, ask at the counter.
  • Overseas cards usually work, but not always. Visa and Mastercard are widely accepted. If your card is rejected, try another card or order at the counter.
  • Leave the card in until the screen tells you. Insert it the way the picture next to the slot shows, and wait until you see ‘카드를 빼 주세요’ (remove your card).
  • You can always go back. Most screens have ‘이전’ (back) and ‘처음으로’ (start over). If you are stuck, staff are usually nearby. Say ‘도와주세요’ (dowajuseyo, please help).

1. Ordering at a cafe

Most cafe kiosks follow the same order. The Korean on each button is shown in brackets.

  1. Tap the screen to startTap anywhere on the start screen, or tap the ‘주문하기’ (order) button.
  2. Choose eat here or take out‘매장’ means eat here and ‘포장’ means take out. Some kiosks ask this at the end instead.
  3. Pick a drinkMenus are split into tabs such as ‘커피’ (coffee), ‘음료’ (drinks) and ‘디저트’ (desserts). Items marked ‘품절’ are sold out.
  4. Choose the optionsA window opens for HOT (따뜻한) or ICE (차가운), size, an extra shot (샷 추가) or syrup. Options marked ‘필수’ (required) must be chosen. Then tap ‘담기’ (add to cart).
  5. Check your order and payTap ‘결제하기’ (pay). If you are asked about points (적립) or coupons and do not need them, tap ‘건너뛰기’ (skip) or ‘안 함’. A dessert offer may pop up too: ‘필요 없어요’ or ‘선택 안 함’ means no thanks.
  6. Pay by cardChoose ‘카드 결제’ (pay by card), insert your card and wait. If you are asked about installments (할부), choose ‘일시불’ (pay in full).
  7. Take your receipt and order numberChoose whether to print a receipt (영수증 출력). Remember your order number (주문번호). It is shown on a screen or called out when your drink is ready.

2. Ordering at a burger shop

Fast-food kiosks add a few more choices, mainly about meals.

  1. Burger only or a meal‘단품’ is the burger only. ‘세트’ is a meal with a side (사이드) and a drink (음료). Some shops also offer a large meal (라지 세트 or 큰 세트).
  2. Choose the side and the drinkFries (감자튀김) and cola (콜라) are usually the default. Some choices cost extra, and the price difference is shown on the button.
  3. Change ingredients if you wantLook for a button such as ‘재료 변경’ or ‘재료 바꾸기’. ‘빼기’ means without (for example, 양파 빼기 = no onion), and ‘추가’ means extra.
  4. Say no to extra offersAfter you add a burger, a dessert or upgrade offer often appears. Tap ‘선택 안 함’ (do not choose) or ‘아니요’ (no) to skip it.
  5. Pay and wait for your numberPay by card as at a cafe. For take-out, watch the order screen: your number moves from ‘준비 중’ (preparing) to ‘완료’ or ‘준비 완료’ (ready). Some restaurants give you a buzzer (진동벨) instead. You may be asked to enter its number on the kiosk, and you pick up your food when it vibrates.

3. Hospital kiosks

Many hospitals and clinics in Korea have kiosks for check-in (접수), payment (수납) and printing papers (증명서 발급). A few things are different for visitors from abroad:

  • Bring your ID. Hospitals in Korea check your identity, so bring your passport or residence card (외국인등록증).
  • On your first visit, go to the desk. Kiosks usually ask for a Korean registration number, or your birth date and phone number. If you are new to the hospital, it is easier to register at the reception desk (원무과 or 접수).
  • Kiosks help on later visits and for payment. Once you are registered, you can check in, pay and print papers at a kiosk.
  • Take the prescription to a pharmacy. A prescription (처방전) is used at a pharmacy (약국). In Korea it is usually valid for 3 days from the issue date, so check the date printed on it.
  • In an emergency, call 119. Travelers can call the Korea Travel Hotline 1330 for information and interpretation in English.

Typical check-in steps

  1. Choose ‘접수’ (check-in) on the first screen.
  2. Choose a first visit (처음 방문 or 초진) or a return visit (재진).
  3. Confirm your identity (본인 확인) by entering numbers or scanning an ID card.
  4. Choose your symptoms or a department (진료과), such as 내과 (internal medicine).
  5. Check the details, take your number ticket (번호표) and wait until your number is called.

4. Airport bus ticket kiosks

At Korean airports, buses to the city and to other regions leave from platforms just outside the arrivals area. You can buy a ticket at a kiosk or at the ticket office (매표소) near the exit. On some routes you can also pay with a transport card when you get on.

  1. Buy a ticket, or print one you bookedOn the first screen, tap ‘승차권 구매’ (buy ticket). If you booked online, tap ‘예매 발권’ (print booking) instead and find your booking with your booking number or phone number. Check the date, choose today’s trip and your ticket is printed. You do not pay again.
  2. Choose your destinationDestinations are often grouped into tabs such as ‘서울’ (Seoul), ‘경기’ (Gyeonggi) and ‘지방’ (other regions). Each one shows a bus number. Check the name of your stop or hotel area first.
  3. Choose a departure timePick a departure time (출발 시각). ‘매진’ means sold out, so choose a later bus. The number of seats left (잔여석 or 남은 좌석) is often shown.
  4. Choose the number of passengersSet adults (성인) and children (어린이) with the + and − buttons. Child fares usually apply to children under 13, but this can differ by bus company.
  5. Choose your seatsOn the seat map, tap one empty seat (빈자리) for each passenger. Window seats (창가) are on the far left and far right. To sit together, choose two seats next to each other with no aisle between them. If none are left, try a later bus. Some short city routes have no seat numbers.
  6. Check and payCheck the destination, time, platform (승차장) and seats. Then tap ‘결제하기’ (pay) and pay by card.
  7. Go to the platformYour ticket shows a platform number such as 4B. Be there about 10 minutes before departure, put big bags in the luggage space under the bus, and show your ticket when you get on.

5. Subway ticket machines

In Korea you can ride the subway with a rechargeable transit card (교통카드), sold at convenience stores and stations, or with a single journey ticket (1회용 교통카드) from a machine in the station. Newer machines take both cash and cards for single journey tickets, but topping up a transit card is usually cash only, so keep some 1,000 won bills.

  1. Choose single journey ticketOn the machine, tap ‘1회용 교통카드’. Many machines also have English, Japanese and Chinese buttons.
  2. Choose your destinationFind the station where you get off by line (호선), for example ‘2호선’ for Line 2, or by its name. The fare depends on the distance.
  3. Choose how many ticketsSet the number of tickets (장수 or 매수). Each ticket costs the fare plus a 500 won deposit (보증금).
  4. PayChoose cash (현금) or card (카드 결제). With cash, insert bills until the amount on the screen is paid, then take your ticket and your change (거스름돈). Some older machines take cash only.
  5. Tap at the gateTap the ticket on the gate reader (개찰구) when you go in and again when you go out.
  6. Get your deposit backAfter your trip, put the ticket into the deposit refund machine (보증금 환급기) near the gates. A 500 won coin comes out.

Topping up a transit card

  1. Tap ‘교통카드 충전’ and place your card on the reader.
  2. Check the balance (잔액) and choose an amount.
  3. Insert cash, and do not remove the card until the screen says it is done.

6. Train ticket machines

At train stations you can buy tickets for high-speed trains (such as KTX and SRT) and regular trains at ticket machines, and most machines have an English button. Some machines do not take cards issued abroad. At big stations such as Seoul Station, look for a machine that takes foreign cards, or buy at the ticket counter.

  1. Choose buy ticketTap ‘승차권 구입’ (buy ticket). ‘예약 승차권 발권’ is for printing a ticket you booked online.
  2. Choose where, when and whoThe departure station is usually the station you are in. Choose the arrival station (도착역), the date and the time you want to leave after, then the number of adults (어른), children (어린이) and seniors (경로, 65 and over). Children and seniors usually get a discount, and seniors may be asked to show ID on the train.
  3. Choose a train and a classYou will see a list of trains with departure and arrival times. Choose standard (일반실) or first class (특실). ‘매진’ means sold out, so choose another train. When every seat is sold out, standing tickets (입석) are sometimes sold.
  4. Choose your seatsLet the machine choose, or pick seats on a seat map. Window seats are on the window side (창측) and the others on the aisle side (내측). Some seats face backward (역방향). If you want to face the direction of travel, choose ‘순방향’.
  5. Pay and take your ticketPay by card and take your ticket. It shows the train number, your car number (호차) and your seat.
  6. Find your platform and carFind your train number on the station board to see the platform (타는 곳). On the platform, wait by your car number, which is marked on the floor or a pillar.

9. When something goes wrong

My card was rejected.
Some kiosks accept only Korean cards. Try another card, or order and pay at the counter.
I cannot find English.
Not every kiosk has a language button. Use the word list above, or ask staff for help.
The kiosk asks for a phone number.
This is usually for points (적립). You can skip it with ‘건너뛰기’ or ‘안 함’.
I chose the wrong thing.
Use ‘이전’ (back) or ‘처음으로’ (start over). In the cart you can delete items with ‘삭제’ or ‘빼기’.
The screen went back to the start.
Kiosks reset if you do nothing for a while. Just start again.
